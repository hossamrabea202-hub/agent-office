# Round 1 — construction before ornament

This revision applies observed construction principles to the fantasy Obsidian Sovereign design. It is not a reconstruction of one historical harness or an assertion of historical accuracy.

## Sources inspected

- [The Met, German breastplate, c.1480, 48.149.32](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/24796): museum photograph and description. Outward-turned borders, a raised lower plate rising to the middle, shallow parallel ridges, and attachment rivets. Used for the integrated lower-plate transition, rim treatment and attachment-boss placement.
- [The Met, German breastplate, c.1500–10, 2014.673](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/667458): description of turned neck and armhole borders, movable gussets, and a four-lame fauld. Used to distinguish breastplate, arm clearance and waist articulation rather than treating the torso as a decorative cylinder.
- [The Met, Giovan Paolo Negroli breastplate, c.1540–45](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/22408): museum photograph and description. Used for foliate ornament arranged within plate fields and for the distinction between raised forms and etched/gilded detail. The original was russet-colored with gilded raised and etched areas; the project retains its own black-and-gold palette.
- [The Met, decoration techniques](https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/techniques-of-decoration-on-arms-and-armor): distinction between embossing, engraving, etching, gilding and colored steel.

## Applied in the model

1. A continuous convex breast rather than paired anatomical muscle bulges; a shallower back and a more defined waist.
2. An outward waist flare and rounded rim relief at the arm and neck openings.
3. A raised lower-plate transition with a central peak and a parallel border, integrated into the same closed shell.
4. Small integral attachment bosses, with local topology refinement rather than loose floating rivet objects.
5. Curved foliate inlay contained above the lower-plate border; restrained lower rays and darker satin steel.

## Limits and next checks

The user's one-connected-element requirement is retained. The lower-plate transition, rims and bosses are integral relief, not separate articulated historical plates. There are no working buckles, opening cuirass mechanism, sliding rivets or functional armhole gussets. These require a construction/skeleton decision in a later iteration.

The high fantasy collar remains from the design sheet. Actual fit over the existing gorget, character proportions, weights, animation clearances and Unreal import still need the private source character. Fine ornament uses high-source shader relief baked onto the low mesh; it is not all separately sculpted geometry. Do not label this revision final, historically accurate or AAA-approved.
