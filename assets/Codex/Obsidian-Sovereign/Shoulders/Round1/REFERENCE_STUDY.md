# Shoulder armour — polish round 1

## Surviving references

- [The Met, pair of German pauldrons, c.1550, 29.150.1g–h](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/34340): museum photograph inspected. The broad main shoulder field, narrow lower lames, edge rolls and attachment rivets informed the design hierarchy. The museum pair is not copied as an exact reconstruction.
- [Worcester Art Museum, right pauldron, c.1540, 2014.1164.6](https://worcester.emuseum.com/objects/50104/right-pauldron): construction description of six lames, shoulder-blade coverage, an armpit cut-away, turned borders and lower-lame edges. Used to replace the original shallow symmetrical dome with a deeper wrap and differentiated front/rear coverage.
- [Cleveland Museum of Art, Italian right pauldron, c.1560–70, 1916.1528](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1916.1528): catalog description of shoulder, armpit, chest and back coverage, blued steel and gilding. Used for the dark-steel/gold treatment and the distinction between broad plate fields and edge decoration.

## Applied changes

The new shoulder is an open wrap of approximately 249 degrees rather than a closed-top bowl. It has a taller main plate, four integral lower-lame reliefs, shorter front skirt, broader rear coverage, rounded border relief, small integral attachment bosses and a restrained central rosette. The main decorative field is kept above the lame transitions. The satin steel palette matches the revised chest.

Both outer and inner sheets are constructed explicitly and joined along their shared boundary. The nominal radial separation is 2.25 mm; normal thickness varies with slope. Fine engraved ornament is high-source shader relief, baked to the final game UVs.

## Limits

One connected, closed shell per shoulder is retained at the user's request. The lower lames and bosses are integral surface forms; they do not have sliding rivets, internal leathers or independent articulation. The mirrored pair suits the fantasy design, whereas historical pieces can be asymmetrical for their intended combat use.

The actual gorget, character and arm poses are still unavailable. Fit, attachment, raised-arm clearance, skinning and Unreal import remain unverified. The first pass is preserved in the parent folder. This revision is WIP and is not an assertion of historical accuracy or AAA completion.
